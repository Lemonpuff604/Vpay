Why this matters
Most “fintech accounts” are pooled.
Pooled wallet structures can create operational and platform risk: commingled funds, harder reconciliation, and unclear ownership in edge cases. Dedicated named accounts reduce that risk by design.
POOLED WALLET MODEL
Master platform account ├─ Employer A “balance” ├─ Employer B “balance” ├─ Employer C “balance” └─ Worker payouts (ledger entries)
Risk: commingled custody + complex reconciliation + platform dependency.
NAMED ACCOUNT MODEL
Bank partner(s) ├─ Employer A (named account) ├─ Employer B (named account) ├─ Employer C (named account) └─ Worker accounts (named)
Outcome: clear ownership + cleaner books + simpler audits.

FAQ
Questions buyers ask on the first call.
If you’re comparing providers, these are the questions that determine whether you have a “wallet product” or true banking infrastructure.
What do you mean by “named accounts” vs “pooled wallets”? +

“Named accounts” means each employer and worker is provisioned a dedicated account structure (clear ownership and reporting), rather than balances tracked inside a single pooled platform account.

Do end users need to use crypto? +

No. Stablecoin settlement can be used behind the scenes where supported. Your UI can remain fiat-native while benefiting from faster settlement options.

What compliance responsibilities sit with us vs you? +

Responsibilities depend on jurisdiction, corridor, and program design. We support KYB/KYC onboarding and monitoring workflows and align the operating model with your compliance requirements.

How fast can we pilot? +

A typical path is: sandbox access → solution design → pilot. Timelines vary by corridor, entity type, and compliance approvals, but we design for fast iteration.
